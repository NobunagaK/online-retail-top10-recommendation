# CS5481 Project 2 | Research and development plan for online retail product recommendations

[English](CS5481_Project2_Research_Development_Plan_3.4.en.md) · [简体中文](CS5481_Project2_Research_Development_Plan_3.4.md)

**Project 2 · Online Retail Recommendation · v3.4 · 2026-11-22**

Given a customer's purchase history before a cutoff, rank 10 products for a later target month. A product is relevant if at least one unit purchased in that month survives the matched credits recorded before month end. The experiment compares **MF, LightGBM, LRURec and Mamba4Rec**. Each model trains independently and scores the same customers and candidate items. All four participate in validation-based model selection and final testing.

**Course requirements and project status.** Page 3 of the project PDF says “exactly three.” A team member confirmed with the instructor that three is the minimum and additional models are allowed. The team retained MF from the lectures, the specified LightGBM model and the recent LRURec model. It also included Mamba4Rec from the list of recent algorithms. The team has checked the course materials, original workbook and upstream source code. Exception analysis, row-level cleaning and basket aggregation have been reproduced locally on the full workbook. D1 exploratory analysis covers paid merchandise in baskets completed from 1 December 2010 through 30 September 2011. D2 builds the shared histories, candidates, queries and separate training/validation labels on CPU. D3–D5 develop all four model implementations first. Acceptance then proceeds one model at a time in the order MF, LightGBM, LRURec and Mamba4Rec. MF, LightGBM and the authors’ LRURec have CPU pilot records. The current D5 implementations instantiate the authors’ pinned model classes. Both sequence models completed Colab Tesla T4 smoke validation. D6 is complete. October validation selected LightGBM before November scoring. D7 will use the frozen predictions for segment and case analysis. The months, budgets and scoring rules below are the team's research design.

### 3. Chronological splits, candidate items and labels

The course requires chronological splits and asks teams to compute customer histories, item popularity and other features from training data. This version retains the previous design based on full calendar months. Each interval includes its start and excludes its end.

**Training samples.** Create snapshots at the start of each month from February through September 2011. Each query `(customer_id, target_month)` requires a purchase history before the start of the target month. The snapshot catalog `I(q)` contains only valid products already observed at that point. The positive set `Y_q` is the intersection of `I(q)` and the distinct valid items purchased in the target month. Keep only training queries with nonempty `Y_q` and count excluded customers and new products separately. All four models share queries, catalogs and positives. MF and LightGBM also share training negatives. For each target month, calculate net-positive basket events at its exclusive end, then take distinct customer–SKU pairs. A full offset removes the associated line. The label remains positive if another qualifying target-month purchase of that SKU survives. A later credit cannot revise a frozen training or evaluation label.

**Histories for validation and test.** Both forecasts use completed baskets and credits observed strictly before `history_end = 2011-10-01 00:00`. October purchases supply validation labels. They enter neither the November inputs nor a model refit. For a customer or customer–SKU pair, `days_since_last_purchase` is the elapsed time in fractional days from the last retained basket to `history_end`. Each rolling count covers `[history_end − N days, history_end)`, for N = 7, 30 or 90. A purchase at noon on September 30 has recency 0.5 days for both forecasts. Historical features for the same key are therefore identical, although different customers can qualify each month.

**Forecast horizon and seasonal demand.** October is the next full month after the common history cutoff (T+1). November is the second (T+2). If their eventual ranking scores differ, the longer horizon, a shift in customer or item mix, and purchases ahead of Christmas are possible explanations. Report the horizons and cohort coverage alongside scores. One validation month and one test month cannot separate those effects.

**Delayed credits and right-censoring.** Monthly labels freeze at their exclusive target end for every split. The audit counts distinct (month, customer, SKU) positives before candidate and cohort filters, then rechecks that purchase month's balances using credits observed through 9 December at 12:50. In February–September, 653/203,020 original month-end positives disappear. In October, 105/43,978 disappear. September alone accounts for 149/37,001. ### 5. Tuning, freezing, and final testing

**Common metrics.** Compute the two primary ranking metrics over the full candidate catalog for all four models. `Recall@10 = number of distinct relevant items in the Top-10 / |Y|`, where $Y$ is the set of distinct eligible items purchased by that customer in the target month. NDCG@10 gives a hit at rank $r$ a weight of $1/\log_2(r+1)$, then divides the sum by the ideal discounted sum with $\min(10,|Y|)$ relevant items ranked first. Compute both metrics for each customer–month query, then average with equal weight per query. Return distinct Top-10 items and break score ties using a fixed `StockCode` order.

**ROC AUC on sampled customer–item pairs (supplementary metric).** The assignment permits this additional metric. Both archived evaluation tables include it. For each customer–month query, retain every item in $Y$ as a positive and sample negatives uniformly without replacement from the same catalog's items with no observed purchase that month. For $P$ positives and $M$ remaining candidates, draw $\min(10P,M)$ distinct negatives once. Determine the sample using a stable item order, a separate sampling seed `auc_sample_seed=5481`, the split, and `query_id`. Save one manifest each for validation and testing. All four models and all training seeds share the manifest for the corresponding split. Generate the test manifest after freezing `selection.json`.

**Calculation and interpretation of AUC.** Use raw item scores before Top-10 selection. Within a query, compare every positive with every sampled negative. Award 1 when the positive scores higher, 0.5 for a tie, and 0 when it scores lower. Average these comparisons within the query, then average equally over queries that have both positives and negatives. Label the column `Sampled ROC AUC (query-macro)`. Count single-class queries separately and omit them only from the AUC mean. Record `NA` if the entire split has no eligible queries. AUC describes the model's ability to distinguish purchases from items with no observed purchase. Use the validation ranking rules in the table below and step 3 for early stopping, tuning, and final selection.
