**Project 2 · Online Retail Recommendation · v3.4 · 2026-11-19**

Given a customer's purchase history before a cutoff, recommend 10 products for a specified later month. The experiment compares **MF, LightGBM, LRURec and Mamba4Rec**. Each model trains independently and scores the same customers and candidate items. All four participate in validation-based model selection and final testing.

**Course requirements and project status.** Page 3 of the project PDF says “exactly three.” A team member confirmed with the instructor that three is the minimum and additional models are allowed. The team retained MF from the lectures, the specified LightGBM model and the recent LRURec model. It also included Mamba4Rec from the list of recent algorithms. The team has checked the course materials, original workbook and upstream source code. Exception analysis, row-level cleaning and basket aggregation have been reproduced locally on the full workbook. D1 exploratory analysis covers paid merchandise in baskets completed from 1 December 2010 through 30 September 2011. D2 is next and will build the cutoff-specific histories, queries, candidates and labels. Model compatibility on Colab, evaluation coverage, runtimes and scores still require experimental runs. The months, budgets and scoring rules below are the team's research design.

### 3. Chronological splits, candidate items and labels

The course requires chronological splits and asks teams to compute customer histories, item popularity and other features from training data. This version retains the previous design based on full calendar months. Each interval includes its start and excludes its end.

| Purpose | Time window | Permitted information |
|---|---|---|
| Training | `[2010-12-01, 2011-10-01)` | Fit models and build history snapshots, vocabularies and training statistics. |
| Validation | `[2011-10-01, 2011-11-01)` | October transactions supply validation labels only, for early stopping, tuning and final model selection. |
| Final test | `[2011-11-01, 2011-12-01)` | Open November labels after freezing configurations and report test metrics for all four models. |
| Partial final month | `[2011-12-01, 2011-12-10)` | The last observed transaction was on 9 December at 12:50. Record the days covered and row count. Use the preceding full months for the main experiment. |

**Training samples.** Create snapshots at the start of each month from February through September 2011. Each query `(customer_id, target_month)` requires a purchase history before the start of the target month. The snapshot catalog `I(q)` contains only valid products already observed at that point. The positive set `Y_q` is the intersection of `I(q)` and the distinct valid items purchased in the target month. Keep only training queries with nonempty `Y_q` and count excluded customers and new products separately. All four models share queries, catalogs and positives. MF and LightGBM also share training negatives.

**Histories for validation and test.** Both use only transactions **before 2011-10-01**. October validation transactions stay out of November customer histories, item popularity, sequences and feature fitting, and out of any training refit. Days since last purchase can be computed from the known target-month start and the last training-period purchase date. The 7-, 30- and 90-day history windows all end at the permitted history cutoff. Testing forecasts farther ahead than validation. The report should explain the combined effects of the change in month and the older history.

**Evaluation population.** The shared candidate set `I_train` includes all valid products observed in the cleaned training period. Customers may receive recommendations for products they have bought before. For each window, select customers who appeared in at least one valid training query and bought at least one candidate that month. For the primary metrics, the relevant set `Y` is the intersection of actual monthly purchases and `I_train`, counting repeated purchases of the same item once per month. All four models use the same query and candidate lists within each window. Score candidates individually or in batches, and include every candidate in the ranking.

**Catalogue items without training signal.** The final training snapshot is on 1 September, while the evaluation catalog covers data through 30 September. The five-rule baseline contains 3,508 training-period stock codes. The frozen merchandise rules give a training catalog of **3,501 products**, of which **114 first appear in September**. The full-workbook count of 3,658 also includes products first seen after the training cutoff. These 114 products were absent from all earlier training candidate sets but can occur in validation inputs and candidates. Preserve their original IDs, purchase histories and eligibility as candidates. For MF, LRURec and Mamba4Rec, record which rows in each item parameter table actually received training signal through an input or a loss calculation. Use the union for shared matrices and exclude PAD. When scoring validation or test queries, replace unsupported embeddings and item biases with the means of supported item rows in their respective tables. Apply the same rule to sequence inputs. LightGBM scores each item using the historical features available by the cutoff. Record the numbers of affected items, positives and hits. Compute all three metrics from the same scores.

**Coverage statistics.** For each window, report active customers, customers unseen in training, customers with a history but no valid training query, customers who bought only out-of-catalog items, and training customers with no purchases that month. In the main table, report the fraction of all active customers eligible for evaluation. Also compute supplementary Recall@10 for every customer with a valid training query and any purchase in the month. Its denominator includes all their distinct valid items that month. Customers buying only out-of-catalog items score zero. Average equally over customers in this group. Report separately the share of out-of-catalog positives and the catalog items that lack training signal.

For example, suppose a customer bought A before the end of September and then bought A, B and B in October. If both A and B are in the training catalog, the October labels are `{A, B}`. A is a valid repeat purchase, and B counts once as a relevant item. If B first appears in valid purchase records in October, the primary metrics use `{A}`. B still contributes to new-item coverage and to the all-item denominator for supplementary Recall.

**LightGBM features and groups.** Customer features include order count, average basket size, spending, and time of most recent purchase. **Shared data and training objective for the sequential models**

1. Build reusable histories keyed by `(customer_id, history_end)`. Order baskets by invoice time and break ties by normalized invoice number. Within each basket, sort items by `StockCode` for reproducibility. Count an item once per invoice and retain repeat purchases across invoices. Exclude the padding ID and out-of-catalog items before normalization. Previously purchased items remain eligible candidates.

$$
\mathcal L(q)=-\frac{1}{|Y_q|}\sum_{i\in Y_q}\log\frac{\exp(s_{q,i})}{\sum_{j\in I(q)}\exp(s_{q,j})}
$$

### 5. Tuning, freezing, and final testing

**Common metrics.** Compute the two primary ranking metrics over the full candidate catalog for all four models. `Recall@10 = number of distinct relevant items in the Top-10 / |Y|`, where $Y$ is the set of distinct eligible items purchased by that customer in the target month. NDCG@10 gives a hit at rank $r$ a weight of $1/\log_2(r+1)$, then divides the sum by the ideal discounted sum with $\min(10,|Y|)$ relevant items ranked first. Compute both metrics for each customer–month query, then average with equal weight per query. Return distinct Top-10 items and break score ties using a fixed `StockCode` order.

**ROC AUC on sampled customer–item pairs (supplementary metric).** The assignment permits this additional metric. The team will include it in validation and final testing. For each customer–month query, retain every item in $Y$ as a positive and sample negatives uniformly without replacement from the same catalog's items with no observed purchase that month. For $P$ positives and $M$ remaining candidates, draw $\min(10P,M)$ distinct negatives once. Determine the sample using a stable item order, a separate sampling seed `auc_sample_seed=5481`, the split, and `query_id`. Save one manifest each for validation and testing. All four models and all training seeds share the manifest for the corresponding split. Generate the test manifest after freezing `selection.json`.

**Calculation and interpretation of AUC.** Use raw item scores before Top-10 selection. Within a query, compare every positive with every sampled negative. Award 1 when the positive scores higher, 0.5 for a tie, and 0 when it scores lower. Average these comparisons within the query, then average equally over queries that have both positives and negatives. Label the column `Sampled ROC AUC (query-macro)`. Count single-class queries separately and omit them only from the AUC mean. Record `NA` if the entire split has no eligible queries. AUC describes the model's ability to distinguish purchases from items with no observed purchase. Use the validation ranking rules in the table below and step 3 for early stopping, tuning, and final selection.

**LightGBM early stopping.** Supply all candidates and `eval_group` for each validation customer–month. Set `eval_at=(10,)` and monitor one validation metric. First use an example with tied scores to compare tie handling and the macro-average of built-in NDCG against the project evaluator. If they differ, disable the built-in metric and use the project's NDCG@10 evaluation function and an early-stopping callback. Use the best iteration consistently for prediction, saving, and reloading. [LightGBM ranking parameters](https://lightgbm.readthedocs.io/en/stable/pythonapi/lightgbm.LGBMRanker.html) · [Early-stopping callback](https://lightgbm.readthedocs.io/en/stable/pythonapi/lightgbm.early_stopping.html)

### 12. Implementation checks that affect grading

| Check | Assertions to verify on small samples |
|---|---|
| Time and label isolation | Every feature event has a timestamp `< history_end`. Training label windows end by the training cutoff. Appending validation or test transactions leaves training features, candidates, and negative samples unchanged. Changing transactions after a training month's cutoff leaves the historical inputs before that cutoff unchanged. |
| Baskets and repeat purchases | Shuffling rows within a basket yields identical aggregated events, sequences, and monthly labels. Cover invoices with multiple timestamps, rows with different unit prices, and a repeat purchase where the history is `[A,B,A]` and the target includes A. When A receives the highest score, all four evaluation paths must rank it first. |
| Customers, candidates, and negatives | For each window, the four models have the same `query_id` values, positive-label hash, item order, and candidate hash. Training negative sampling excludes every positive for the target month. Count cases with no purchases, cases with no candidate positives, and cold-start cases separately. Use a synthetic item first observed in September to check history encoding, retention in the full candidate set, and fallback scores for parameters without training support. |
| Shared sequences and monthly labels | Use histories of length one, histories with repeat purchases, and histories longer than `L` to check valid IDs, the last valid position, and truncation. Fix the weights and turn off dropout. Compare candidate scores for the same history scored alone and in batches with different padded lengths, and state the numerical tolerance. Purchases `A,B,B` in a month yield the target set `{A,B}`. Verify that the loss agrees with the hand calculation. Always exclude the padding ID and items outside the catalog. |
| Ranking and metrics | With ground truth `{B,D}` and a predicted Top-10 of `[A,B,C,E,F,G,H,I,J,K]`, hand calculation gives Recall@10 = 0.5 and NDCG@10 ≈ 0.38685. Also test zero hits, ties, duplicate items, and more than 10 relevant items. |
| Sampled ROC AUC | For one query with positive score 0.8 and negative scores 0.8 and 0.2, AUC should be 0.75. Add a query with positive score 0.1 and negative score 0.3. Their macro average should be 0.375. Check the number of single-class queries excluded, the sample manifest hash across the four models, and reuse of the evaluation manifest when the training seed changes. |
| Training and recovery | MF, LRURec, and Mamba4Rec update their parameters on a small batch. Both sequence models have finite gradients with nonzero entries in their encoders and scoring layers. Scores before and after reloading agree within numerical tolerance. Reloading the LightGBM native model preserves its iteration count and ranking. |
| Freezing and reproducibility | Trace each run to its configuration, seed, hardware, and inputs. The test entry point checks selection records and the protocol for all four models. Verify the means and standard deviations across the three seeds against the individual run records. |

If the team discovers a material implementation error after viewing test results, retain the original run and mark it invalid. Record the changes and affected experiments, and disclose the re-evaluation procedure in the report.
